# A maps section in Sasonica (6 Nov 2026)

David, 6 Nov 2026: "I'd like to discuss adding a maps section to the
sasonica". Asked what it is for, he picked all four offered (agents show
places, where things are, location history, navigation by voice) and
added: "I'd also liked it to overlay other information like speed cameras
and mobile phone detection, cameras and things like that". Status:
**steps 1 and 2 built (9 Nov 2026), step 3's OSM cameras 10 Nov; the rest proposed.**

## The shape: one map, many layers

A single Map page in the app. The server says which layers exist and
serves each as GeoJSON. The app draws what it is sent and knows nothing
about where the data comes from (docs/decisions/2026-09-21-app-stays-generic.md).

- `GET /map/layers` → `[{id, name, kind: points|lines|track|route, icon?,
  colour?, default_on, refresh_s?, private?}]`
- `GET /map/layers/<id>?bbox=w,s,e,n` → a GeoJSON FeatureCollection, each
  feature with `title`, `detail?` and `link?` (a thread, a device, a URL).
- A layer source is a small server-side plugin (a Python entry point, as
  the personal-use add-ons are), so a region's camera feed or a house's
  floor plan lives in an add-on or on the host and never in this repo
  (no host config in agent-media).

**Drawing.** MapLibre GL JS (BSD-3) in the WebView, with vector tiles.
For tiles: start with a hosted OpenStreetMap-based provider (OpenFreeMap
needs no key), with a self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles file of Victoria on
red5 as the fallback, served by the server over public HTTPS (not the
tailnet). On the Pine Note: a greyscale line-art style, no fills or
animation.

## The layers

1. **Agent places.** A reply carries `[[map: …]]` (pins, a route, a box),
   as replies carry `[[visual: …]]` now. The thread shows a small map
   inline. Tapping it opens the Map page with that reply's layer on. Each
   thread's places are kept as a layer (`thread:<id>`) until the thread
   goes.
2. **Where things are.** Devices, rooms and speakers placed once by hand
   (drag a pin, or "the kitchen speaker is here"), plus the phone's live
   dot from §6.21b. A floor plan comes later, as an image overlay on the
   house.
3. **Location history.** The biggest privacy change in this proposal,
   see below.
4. **Overlays from open data.**
   - Fixed speed and red-light cameras: OSM `highway=speed_camera`, plus
     the Victorian government's published list if there is one under an
     open licence (to check: what is published, how often it is updated,
     and the licence).
   - Mobile phone detection cameras: in Victoria these move. To check what
     is published (whether it names sites, a schedule, or nothing). If
     nothing, the layer shows known sites only and says so. It never
     guesses.
   - Surveillance cameras: OSM `man_made=surveillance` (Overpass query,
     cached daily per region).
   - Each overlay shows how old its data is. A camera layer never claims
     to be complete.
   - Legality: camera alerts are legal in Victoria (Waze and others do
     it). To check before any public build in other places; some
     countries ban them (e.g. France, Switzerland, parts of Germany).
     Public builds get a per-region switch, set on the server.
5. **Navigation by voice.** Last, as it is the largest piece:
   - A routing engine on red5: Valhalla (MIT) or GraphHopper (Apache-2.0)
     on the Victoria extract. OSRM is lighter but car-first.
   - The phone sends its position while a route is on. The server works
     out the next instruction and speaks it through the usual speech
     path, ducked under music and held during a call (call_guard).
   - Camera alerts are spoken on the route ("speed camera in 300
     metres") from layer 4.
   - Audio-first: designed to be used without looking, as in
     `2026-11-05-audio-first-messenger.md`.

## Location history and privacy

Today the phone's shared location is kept **in memory only**, never
written to disk, newest per phone (§6.21b). That was chosen on purpose
(David, 20 Oct 2026: "location data … as an optional extra with the
default being off"). History has to change it, so:

- It is a separate switch, off by default: "Keep where I've been".
- It is stored on the user's own server only, in its own file
  (`location.db`), never synced to the long-term memory store or sent to
  any model provider. An agent reads it through a tool, and only the
  answer goes into the chat ("you were at Southland on Tuesday, about
  2 pm").
- Points are thinned on the phone: a point when it has moved more than
  about 50 m, or every few minutes while still. Stays are grouped into
  visits (place, from, to).
- Kept for 90 days by default (30 days, one year or forever in Settings), with "forget today"
  and "forget everything" in Settings.
- Battery: the Android fused provider at balanced power, and only while
  moving. To measure on the p8a before turning it on for anyone.
- Later, with it: reminders tied to a place ("remind me when I'm at
  Bunnings"), and the music bed and briefing knowing whether you are
  home, out or driving.

## Order to build

1. The Map page, `/map/layers`, tiles, and the phone's live dot. **Built 6–9 Nov
   2026** (server §6.50, `map_layers.py`; app `routes/maps.tsx`).
2. Agent places (`[[map: …]]`), inline in threads. **Built 9 Nov 2026**: `[[map:]]`
   and `[[route:]]` with coordinates from `media place find` (no geocoding at
   display time), a card under the reply, the chat's places as a Map layer.
3. Camera overlays (OSM first, then Victorian government data once checked).
   **OSM built 10 Nov 2026**: Speed cameras and CCTV layers, read for the
   box on screen. Public Overpass refuses a cloud host, so red5 keeps them
   from a weekly Geofabrik Australia file (`map_osm.py refresh`). Victoria
   publishes fixed camera sites, a monthly list of approved mobile camera
   sites, and a monthly list of phone and seatbelt camera sites
   (data.vic.gov.au, CC BY): still to do, as an add-on, since some lists
   give addresses that need geocoding.
4. Where things are (devices, rooms, speakers). **Built 22 Nov 2026**:
   `GET/POST /map/things` (`map_things.py`, §6.50), per account, the ids the
   server already knows (paired devices, the music stream's rooms, audio
   targets that are places), with their state where cheap; the built-in
   layer "Your things"; the app draws each as a key with a card (a room's
   switch and volume, play/pause), "Place your things" in Layers (pick, then
   tap the map or Here), a long press to move or remove; `media place thing
   <kind> <name> --here` for "the kitchen speaker is here". The floor plan
   is still to come.
5. Location history and its Settings. **Built 22 Nov 2026**: the phone's
   "Keep where I've been" (thinned on the phone, batched to `POST
   /device/location/history`), `location_history.py` with its own
   `location.db` (visits, names from saved places, things or one reverse
   lookup, keep 30/90/365/forever, forget a day or all; not in backups),
   `GET /map/history*` (§6.50), `media location where-was-i` and the
   `location_history` MCP tool answering in words; the app's Settings →
   Location and the Map's Timeline.
6. Navigation by voice. **Decided 12 Nov 2026, waiting for red2** (David:
   "let's hold off on this until we're on red2"): the routing engine runs
   on red2 once it is the server (docs/proposals/2026-11-12-the-server-moves-home.md),
   not on red5 or a hosted service. The first version is a route on the
   Map with spoken turns as you go, rerouting when you leave it, camera
   alerts alongside. Where to: a search box on the Map, by voice ("directions
   to Brunetti"), and a Directions key on a reply's places and the Map's pins.
   **Started 17 Nov 2026 on red2.** Open source first: Valhalla (MIT) over
   GraphHopper (Apache-2.0, Java, its turn instructions less complete) and
   OSRM (BSD, car-first, walking weaker): Valhalla routes walking and
   driving from one set of tiles and writes the spoken words of each turn
   itself (`verbal_pre_transition_instruction`), in many languages. It runs
   from PyPI's `pyvalhalla` (engine and `valhalla_service` included, no
   system packages) in `~/.local/share/valhalla` on red2, tiles built from
   Geofabrik's Australia extract.
   **Built 18 Nov 2026**: Valhalla as `agent-media-valhalla` on red2
   (127.0.0.1:8002, tiles in 4 min), `GET /map/route` and `/map/search`
   (agent-media 801b2bf7), and the app's "Where to?" box, Walk / Drive /
   Bike, the route line and turns, guidance with spoken turns and
   rerouting, and Directions keys on places (sasonica-app e2320820; spoken
   on the phone by `PhoneLocation.say`, NavVoice.java, the camera alerts'
   voice). Still to do: a Directions key on the map's pins themselves, and
   rebuilding the tiles weekly with the camera refresh.
   **Route planner options, 19 Nov 2026** (David: "let's add the route
   planner options that Google maps has"): `GET /map/route` takes `avoid`
   (tolls, highways, ferries), `alternates` (up to two), `via` stops (a leg
   each), `depart` / `arrive` and `mode=transit`, and answers `routes` (the
   chosen first), `legs`, each step's `leg` and, on a ride, `transit` (line,
   headsign, times, stops); `GET /map/route/modes` lists Transit only where
   the service has transit tiles (§6.50). All of it is Valhalla's own:
   costing options, alternates, `date_time` and its multimodal costing. The
   transit tiles come from PTV's GTFS Schedule (CC BY 4.0, outside-services
   row 62), built on red2 by `valhalla_ingest_transit` and
   `valhalla_convert_transit` before the road tiles; open source first, no
   separate transit planner (OpenTripPlanner, MOTIS) was needed, since
   Valhalla already routes walk-ride-walk from the same tiles.

   add a whole lot of features based on Google maps"; all four groups
   chosen, in this order). Built so far, 19 Nov: guidance that carries on in
   other sections with a floating map (sasonica-app f19d5439, 8f154efa);

   (`GET /map/recent_places`, 9f5403b1; app 64b3f76f); Home and Work keys
   and places of a kind nearby (`GET/POST /map/saved`, 22ac9c9c, and

   1. **Saved lists and stars.** Star a place or put it in lists of one's

      gains `lists` on each place and `/map/saved/lists`.
   2. **A place's card.** A tap on a pin or a result opens a card: name,
      kind, opening hours and open now, phone, website, then Directions,
      Save, Share. From what `/map/places` and `/map/search` already give,
      OSM's tags; no paid place data.
   3. **Share a trip.** While guiding, a link someone else opens to see the
      phone's position and arrival time live, ended with the trip or by
      hand. Server: a trip token, the phone's fixes (already sent for the
      phone layer), and a public page; off the tailnet by design.
      **Built** (19 Nov: agent-media 76313cbc, app 180ef7c2; 22 Nov: agent-media
      21990bd3, app c29a9a66): the link, how long it lasts, the public page
      at `/trip/<token>` (noindex, the route ahead, whose trip); Send in a
      chat sends it in a Messages chat with a heads-up five minutes out and
      on arrival (step 10's "Share a trip"); gone an hour after the ETA if
      the phone goes quiet.
   4. **Traffic, lanes, offline.** Lane guidance from Valhalla's own
      `lanes` on each maneuver (free, from OSM; **built 22 Nov**, agent-media
      21990bd3, app c29a9a66: Valhalla 3.9 gives lanes only in its OSRM
      form, so the server asks for that too; the app draws them under the
      going banner and in its key). **Traffic and offline built 22 Nov**
      (agent-media 3cdaf006, app 9f4a77b6; §6.50 "Live traffic" and "Offline
      maps"; outside-services rows 74 and 75):
      - *Traffic.* Open data first: DTP's Freeway Travel Time (CC BY 4.0) is
        Melbourne's freeway sensors only, behind a portal key; HERE Traffic
        v7's free allowance could not be confirmed. So TomTom (the key already
        on red2): its flow and incident vector tiles become the Map's Traffic
        layer (green, amber, red on the roads, crashes, closures and roadworks
        as pins; read by the server, kept 3 minutes), and its Routing API,
        asked to follow Valhalla's own line, gives each drive its time in
        today's traffic ("+6 min traffic", the fastest first). Valhalla has no
        live traffic, and adjusting its time by the flow along the line would
        mean matching segments ourselves; asking TomTom for the time alone
        keeps Valhalla's roads and words. While driving, the way ahead is
        timed again every 5 minutes and a way on other roads saving 3 minutes
        and 10% is offered ("Faster route available"). Ceilings inside the
        free tiers: 150,000 of 200,000 tiles and 15,000 of 20,000 routes a
        month (`MEDIA_TOMTOM_TRAFFIC_MONTHLY_MAX`, `…_ROUTING_…`). TomTom's
        terms on showing its traffic over another map are still to read
        before a public build.
      - *Offline.* Victoria (zoom 0–14, 229 MB) cut from Protomaps' daily
        planet PMTiles by range requests on the server (`map_offline.py
        build`, in Python, no Go or Java), with the Protomaps style's fonts
        and sprites; Settings → Maps downloads it over Wi-Fi with Android's
        download manager, and the Map draws it with Protomaps' style when
        there is no signal (or always, when asked), swapping back when the
        signal returns. The route being guided is kept with the trip, so a
        restart with no signal carries on; rerouting needs the server, and
        says so. Camera alerts were already offline-tolerant (paired: the
        last 25 km of cameras in memory; unpaired: a day's on the phone).
        A monthly rebuild timer on red2 (`agent-media-map-offline.timer`
        running `map_offline build`) is still to be installed.
      - *Routing on the phone*, looked at, not built: Valhalla's mobile
        builds (valhalla-mobile, a Kotlin wrapper of the C++ engine; routing
        tiles for Victoria a few hundred MB more, and native libraries per
        CPU) would give the same words offline and is the natural next step;
        GraphHopper (Apache-2.0) runs on Android but its Android port is
        unmaintained and its instructions are thinner; BRouter (MIT) is a
        separate app others call through a service, bikes and walking first.
        Each roughly doubles what the phone keeps and needs its own tile
        build, so the kept route covers the common case (signal lost on a
        route already planned) for now.
   5. **Check the hours, for OpenStreetMap** (built 22 Nov 2026; David:
      "let's also upload it to open street maps", choosing "Both" when told
      OSM forbids copying Google's or Foursquare's). Every place whose card

      (`site_read.py`) into proposed hours and tags, and asked before Google
      on the card; a website that disagrees with OSM's hours lists the place
      as a conflict; passing within 60 m nudges the phone ("Check the hours
      at …?", an alert with `route` `/maps?check=<key>`). Nothing goes to
      OSM until David confirms it in the app, and then with
      `check_date:opening_hours`.
8. **Reviews from open data** (David, 22 Nov 2026: "fuck yeah!", to adding

10. **Sasonica's own on the map** (David, 22 Nov 2026: all chosen):
   - Audio tour: as you walk or drive, the app's voice tells you about what you

     the spot you point at.
   - Trip by chat: an agent plans a day ("the Dandenongs, lunch and a walk") and

   - Share a trip: live position and ETA to someone in Messages (Matrix), a

   - Where I parked: the car's spot kept when the phone leaves the car's
     Bluetooth, and directions back to it.
   - Trip music: the music bed or a podcast sized to the trip's time, ending on

   - Weather along the route: rain and heat at each stop and on the way, from
     Open-Meteo (free, open; already used for the music bed's weather).
   - Hills: an elevation profile for a walk or ride (Valhalla's height service
     with elevation tiles).
   - Sunset, golden hour and dark skies: when and where, for walks and

   - Memories: places from chats and photos on the map, and "a year ago you

   - Offline: tiles and routing on the phone for no signal (Protomaps PMTiles

11. **Life around the map** (David, 22 Nov 2026: all chosen, after step 10):
   - Beaches: swim and water quality (EPA Victoria's Beach Report, open data to
     confirm) and waves and wind (Open-Meteo marine).
   - Rock pools (David, 22 Nov 2026: "when rock pools are exposed ... and any other
     relevant information"): low tides near you with time and height (Open-Meteo
     marine's sea level; the Bureau of Meteorology's tide tables only after reading
     their terms), the ones worth going for (in daylight, the month's lowest around
     new and full moon), a heads-up the day before ("Rock pools at Blairgowrie
     tomorrow: low 0.2 m at 10:40"); with it the swell and waves then (a warning on
     a big-swell day), when the tide turns, marine sanctuary boundaries (Parks
     Victoria open data: no taking), recent sightings nearby (iNaturalist), and

   - Places to practise: quiet parks, lookouts and calm spots near you, tied to
     the practices saved in Media; and a gentle nudge to get outside after
     days indoors (from location history, step 5).
   - Leave now: "leave in 10 minutes for your 3 pm" from a calendar event's

   - Safety and access: a check-in timer for a walk home (a message to someone if
     you are not home by then); step-free, pram-friendly walking routes

12. **Games with the people following a trip** (David, 22 Nov 2026: all chosen).
   The driver only ever listens and speaks: nothing to look at, and nothing said
   while a turn is coming up (guidance first). Followers play from the trip page
   (sharing a trip, step 7) or the Messages chat it was sent to.
   - Guess my arrival: followers bet on the ETA; the winner is said on arrival.
   - Where am I?: followers see a blurred map and guess the town; the driver can
     say a hint; the closest guess is read out.
   - DJ from afar: followers queue songs into the car's music, each said with who
     picked it; name that tune: a snippet plays, followers race to name it.
   - Roadside trivia: a question about a landmark being passed (step 9/10's
     Wikipedia), followers answer in the chat, the driver aloud, a running score;
     a story chain, each adding a line, read back by the agent.
   - Voice notes from followers read out on straight stretches, answered by voice;
     cheers they record, played at halfway and on arrival.

## Decided (David, 6 Nov 2026)

- **Menu:** Map is its own top-level entry, beside Chats and Media.
- **Tiles:** hosted to start (OpenFreeMap). A self-hosted Victoria
  PMTiles file on red5 is added later as the fallback.
- **History kept:** Settings offers 30 days, 90 days, one year and
  forever. The default is 90 days ("have options for all of them in
  settings but default to 90 days").
- **Navigation:** walking and driving together from the start. One
  routing engine serves both, with a mode switch; camera alerts are
  spoken in driving mode only.
