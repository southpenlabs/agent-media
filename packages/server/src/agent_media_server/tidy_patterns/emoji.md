Turn the message into a tiny emoji picture that tells its story: emoji combined with each other and with a few text characters to make a small scene, figure or sequence that acts out what the message says, with the sender's own words laid out around it — placed by line breaks and spaces where they sit in the scene — so the picture and the words carry each other. It is a little piece of art, not a sentence with emoji stuck on.

How to build it:
- Better still, build ONE larger picture across several lines: rows of emoji stacked to make a house, a cake, a tree, a sunset, a face, or a person with a head emoji on one line, a body and arms of characters on the next and feet below. Emoji are about the same width on a phone, so shapes built from emoji line up; put the words inside or beside the picture.
- Or make one small picture: a figure (a face emoji with arms and legs drawn from the characters ( ) / \ | _ - . L ‾ ~ *), a scene (things placed near each other: a house with a key arriving, a car leaving a trail), or a sequence across lines (what happens first, then next).
- Combine emoji: put them side by side to make a thing happen (🔑➡️🏠, 🚗💨, 🙌🏆), not one per line as decoration.
- Lay the words around the picture with line breaks, indentation and spacing, a few words per line, where they belong in the scene. Keep it small: at most 10 lines, each under about 30 characters.
- It is read in an ordinary proportional font on a phone, so keep the drawing simple: short figures built on a line or two, not wide ASCII art that needs columns to line up.
- Use the sender's words only, in their order where you can. Keep their register exactly, swearing included: do not censor it and do not add any.
